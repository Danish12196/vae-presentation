Where autoencoders come from

  1. 1986Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams. The commonly cited origin: train a network to rebuild its input, so a hidden layer has to learn a code. (The backpropagation paper, and the PDP chapter the same year.)

Part 5 · Variational autoencoders

Where VAEs come from

  1. 2013Kingma & Welling, “Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes”. Posted in December 2013, then at ICLR 2014. Encoder outputs a distribution, and the reparameterization trick lets you train it.
  2. 2014Rezende, Mohamed & Wierstra, “Stochastic Backpropagation and Approximate Inference in Deep Generative Models”, ICML 2014. Independent, and almost the same idea, at the same time.

two papers, one trick: keep the randomness where a gradient can get past it

Probability in one minute

analogya table of grades and ages: column totals, and one row rescaled

joint: how likely an image and a code are together.

, marginals: add up the other variable, .

a slice, rescaled: the images one code can make. That is the decoder.

the posterior: which codes explain this image. That is the encoder's job.

For real cats, is an integral over every possible code in 12,288-D: no one can compute it. So the VAE approximates the posterior with a Gaussian . That approximation is the variational in VAE.

Turning the KL dial

Reconstruction KL

loss = −ELBO (Evidence Lower BOund). The true likelihood is intractable, so we maximise this lower bound instead. With a fixed-variance Gaussian decoder, the first term is our L2 up to a constant and a scale.

Part 5 · Variational autoencoders

Backpropagation, briefly

analogythe chain rule is a relay of blame, passed back one layer at a time

  1. Forward. x goes through w₁ and w₂ and comes out as a guess ŷ.
  2. Loss. One number: how wrong the guess was.
  3. Backward. Each box passes the blame back, times its own local slope.
  4. Step. Every weight moves a little downhill: w ← w − η · ∂L/∂w.

loss over (w₁, w₂): dark is low

a plain autoencoder is smooth the whole way, so the blame reaches every weight

Part 5 · Variational autoencoders

A dice roll has no gradient

analogyyou can’t ask a dice how to roll a bit higher

Plain autoencoder

z = encoder(x)

z is a plain function of the weights. The chain rule goes straight through it. Backprop is fine.

VAE, if you sample directly

z ~ N(μ, σ)

μ and σ themselves are fine to differentiate. The random draw is not a smooth function of them, so the gradient stops at the dice. That is the only break.

next: move the dice outside the graph. That is the reparameterization trick.

Part 6 · Limitations, extensions, wrap-up

Measure it, then use it well

analogyFID compares two crowds, not two faces

How do we measure it?

Reconstruction: PSNR on the 64 held-out cats, computed live. Higher is better.

Generation has no answer key, so compare clouds: FID runs real and generated images through a pretrained network and measures how far apart the two clouds of features are. Lower is better. This deck shows reconstruction, not FID.

Where it’s used

  • Anomaly detection: factory defects, odd medical scans
  • Denoising and compression
  • Synthetic data to grow a small dataset
  • Latent space for Stable Diffusion

Use it responsibly

  • It learns the data’s bias. Ours saw only front-facing cats, so a side-on cat looks “abnormal”. With people, that is a fairness problem.
  • Faces that never existed: deepfakes, consent for face data, label synthetic images.

a model you can’t measure is a model you can’t trust

Wrap-up

  1. 1986AutoencoderRumelhart, Hinton & Williams. A bottleneck code. Deep ones in 2006. Denoising in 2008.

  3. 2013VAEKingma & Welling, and Rezende, Mohamed & Wierstra. A cloud you can sample.
  4. 2014GANGoodfellow et al. Sharp, and it can collapse to one picture.
  5. 2015DiffusionSohl-Dickstein et al. 2015, then Ho, Jain & Abbeel’s DDPM in 2020. Many steps, high quality.

β-VAE, the KL dial

β is the weight on the KL term. β = 0 is a plain autoencoder: the codes scatter. β = 1 is an ordinary VAE. β = 4 packs every code toward zero, and the cats get blurrier.

That is the slider on “Turning the KL dial”: beta 0, 0.1, 1 and 4, on the same cats.
